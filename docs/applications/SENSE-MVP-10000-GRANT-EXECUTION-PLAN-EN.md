# SENSE MVP Grant Execution Plan — $10,000 MENACatalyst Prize

## 1. Executive decision

The prize should not be used to build a fully national platform, launch a large marketplace, or activate payments, bookings, delivery, or production NFC infrastructure from day one. It should fund a focused, measurable tourism and commerce MVP starting in Al-Eizariya and testing the following journey:

> Verified story → clear destination → understandable route → local experience or provider → locally connected product → feedback and measurement.

If the pilot performs well, expansion to Bethlehem, Beit Sahour, Birzeit, Salfit and other destinations can be based on evidence rather than on a purely visual expansion of pages.

## 2. Proposed MVP scope

### What the first release will include

- A responsive Arabic RTL mobile-first interface.
- A homepage that explains SENSE within seconds.
- A pilot destination page for Al-Eizariya.
- One reviewable route with no more than 5–7 stops.
- 5–8 story or destination cards, each with verification status, source and review date.
- 3–5 local providers or makers after receiving clear consent.
- 5–10 products or product concepts, without claiming availability or price before confirmation.
- An “I am interested / contact the provider” workflow instead of automated payment or booking.
- A source, rights and consent register.
- Practical accessibility information where field-verified, with clear labels for unknown information.
- A simple measurement dashboard for visits, clicks, contact requests, corrections and user feedback.

### What will not be included in the MVP

- A payment gateway or financial settlement system.
- Live delivery or automated inventory management.
- Confirmed bookings or instant sales.
- Production NFC infrastructure or real NFC keys.
- A separate native mobile application.
- National expansion before the local pilot is validated.
- Claims about users, revenue or impact without evidence.

## 4. Six-month implementation plan

### Month 1: Problem and scope validation — $1,000

Define the visitor, provider and local-maker personas; select one Al-Eizariya route; prepare consent templates for images, audio, stories and products; define success indicators and the baseline; and review the platform’s public name, description and legal information.

**Decision gate:** Do not begin wider development until one route and 5–8 reviewable elements have been approved.

### Month 2: Research and data — $1,600

Conduct short interviews with residents, providers and potential visitors. Document destinations, stories and practical information. Record the source, information owner, review date, access status and confidence level for each item. Select only 3–5 providers after consent, and distinguish verified information from local narratives and design proposals.

**Output:** An organized initial data pack, not just a set of promotional texts.

### Month 3: Design and prototype — $2,000

Design the Arabic RTL mobile experience and the flow from discovery to reading, planning and contacting. Create destination, route and product-card templates. Test the first design with five users before full development. Add the status system: draft, under verification, reviewed, published and archived.

**Decision gate:** Remove any feature that does not help the user take a clear next step.

### Month 4: Development and integration — $2,400

Develop the destination and route pages, add the contact or review-request form, include source, rights and last-review fields, add basic search or filtering, connect the limited catalogue to providers without activating payments or inventory, and test mobile display, keyboard access, links and loading speed.

### Month 5: Content entry and field testing — $1,700

Enter the main story and reviewed cards. Publish 5–10 product or product-concept cards with confirmed information only. Run tests with 10–15 users and 3–5 providers. Measure comprehension, accessibility and clarity of the next step, then fix the ten highest-impact issues.

### Month 6: Limited pilot launch and scale decision — $1,300

Launch a limited public pilot. Publish a short transparency note explaining what works and what remains under verification. Measure usage, contact and correction activity. Document provider and user feedback with consent, then issue a decision report: expand, revise or retest.

## 5. Proposed success indicators

### Concrete outputs

- One published and reviewed route.
- 5–8 destination/story cards with sources and clear statuses.
- 3–5 providers with documented consent.
- 5–10 product or product-concept cards.
- 10–15 documented user tests.
- At least three review sessions with providers or local knowledge holders.
- Known rights status for 100% of published images and stories.
- A final report explaining what was achieved and what was not.

### Initial impact indicators, not advance promises

- Percentage of users who find destination information within two minutes.
- Percentage of users who understand the next step.
- Number of contact requests or referrals to providers.
- Number of corrections or updates submitted by the community.
- Percentage of cards containing practical accessibility information.
- Provider satisfaction with the presentation of their stories and products.
- Percentage of items still up to date after 30 days.

## 6. Spending and governance

The prize should be released in milestone-based instalments rather than spent at once. No media or content cost should be paid without clear rights approval. Each expense should have a supplier, purpose, date, receipt and linked result. Grant funds should not be spent on media production that does not enter the MVP or cannot be reused. Passwords, payment credentials and production NFC keys must never be stored in GitHub.

WordPress should remain the public content and publishing layer, while GitHub remains the source for code, documentation and change history. Every provider or partner should be able to review what is attributed to them before publication.

## 7. Key risks and responses

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Scope expands to several cities | Keep Al-Eizariya as the first case and open new destinations only after measurement |
| Provider consent is unavailable | Use consent forms and delay publication or anonymize the material |
| Historical information is uncertain | Label it as under verification and do not present it as established fact |
| Expectations of a full online store | Use a catalogue and interest form until payment and delivery are operational |
| The project depends on one person | Document tasks, accounts, data and the publishing workflow |
| Development costs increase | Start with a responsive web MVP, not a native app or complex infrastructure |
| User adoption is weak | Test early and remove features that do not lead to a clear action |
| SENSE is confused with Burhan, Battir or Solomon’s Pools | Maintain separate project cards and use one project name in the application |

## 8. The grant promise

The strongest and most credible promise is not to build “the Amazon of Palestinian tourism” in six months. It is:

> With a $10,000 grant, SENSE will build and test an Arabic MVP that connects a destination story to a route, a local provider and a product, starting in Al-Eizariya. The project will produce verified data, consent records, a mobile-ready user flow, user testing and an initial measurement report that determines whether national expansion is justified.

This promise matches the current stage of the venture and positions the prize as an investment in proving the model rather than general funding for an overly broad idea.
