## Day 1 — From a need to a reviewable outcome

A real need deserves more than a fast answer.

SENSE turns a starting need into a researchable question, a set of alternatives, and a first model that can be reviewed before it moves forward.

That is the working path: clarify the need, make the options visible, build a useful first version, and learn from the result.

What would you build if your next project began with one clearly stated need?

## Day 2 — The Work Compass

The Work Compass gives an individual or a team a practical starting point.

Choose the need. Choose the role for AI: research, alternatives, or a model. Choose the verification method: a source, a review, or a trial.

The output is a work card that can be copied, discussed, and improved locally—without turning the process into a black box.

Good tools do more than accelerate work. They make the next decision easier to see.

#SENSETECH #WorkCompass #ResponsibleAI

## Day 3 — National Continuity

Continuity can be built through everyday capabilities: learning, access, memory, and local projects.

The SENSE National Continuity route connects those capabilities with four building layers: sources, skills, organization, and archives.

The aim is practical: help people and teams carry useful knowledge forward, adapt it to a project, and keep the work reviewable as it grows.

A stronger future often starts with preserving the ability to begin again.

## Day 4 — Responsible AI in team work

AI becomes more valuable when people remain able to question, verify, and learn from the work.

SENSE gives AI a defined role inside a human workflow: find relevant material, compare possible paths, help shape a first model, and support a review.

The team keeps the purpose, judgment, and responsibility. The system helps make the path visible.

Build faster—but keep the ability to understand what you built.

#SENSETECH #ResponsibleAI #TeamWork #DigitalSkills

## Day 5 — Urban-Sense municipal workflow

A municipal report needs a visible path from signal to action.

Urban-Sense organizes the core workflow around report intake, review, classification, assignment, field evidence, verification, documented closure, and an audit trail.

The result is a clearer operating model for the people responsible for receiving, processing, and closing civic reports.

The next step is always concrete: make the report understandable, route it to the right role, and keep the record accountable.

#UrbanSense #PublicServices #CivicTechnology #SENSETECH

## Day 6 — SENSE Experience provider pathway

Local providers need a clear way to introduce what they can offer, understand the quality path, and move toward a considered public presence.

SENSE Experience is being developed around a provider-interest route, manual review, separated data boundaries, and a quality conversation before publication.

This creates room for tourism, service, learning, and local-product work to grow with context—one provider, one offer, and one reviewable step at a time.
